## 0. How to read this record

* **Every fact carries its source and page**, using the short names of §1.
  A page number is the document's printed page where it has one that is
  unambiguous, and the PDF page otherwise; §1 says which for each document.
* **Every statement is one of five kinds**, and the page shows the first four
  in words beside each number:
  * **DOCUMENTED** — read in the primary document at the page given, quoted
    or paraphrased closely;
  * **ATTRIBUTED** — a conclusion or estimate a document states as ITS OWN
    (the inquiry commission's, the auditor's); repeated as theirs, never as
    this record's;
  * **CALCULATION** — this record's arithmetic on documented figures, shown
    so it can be redone;
  * **ASSUMPTION** — a teaching value or modelling choice that no document
    read gives;
  * **UNCONFIRMED** — stated somewhere, not found in the primary documents
    read (§9).  Never stated on the page as a fact.
* **Every documented fact is dated by PERIOD**: the DECISION (2006 to the
  approval of the project-finance structure in November 2008);
  CONSTRUCTION AND FINANCING (2009-2011); OPERATION, RESTRUCTURING AND SALE
  (2012-2017); AFTER THE SALE (2018-2025).  Information from a later period
  was not available at the decision, and the page never presents it as if it
  had been.  Retrospective scenarios built on later information are labelled
  as such.
* **Four facts are kept apart for every risk**: the RISK IDENTIFIED, the
  CONDITION RECOMMENDED, the DECISION TAKEN (what the documents record about
  it), and the LATER OUTCOME.  Each has its own date and document; no link
  between them is presumed where no document states one.  A risk documented
  at the time shows that it was identified; it does not show that the size
  of the later losses or the later course of the market was foreseeable.
* **Quotations from Portuguese and Spanish documents are given in the
  original, with an English translation beside them.**  The translations
  are this record's, not the sources'.
* The source documents were downloaded and read as text extracts; they are
  NOT in the tree.  Two carry restrictions worth stating: the EY report's
  own cover marks it confidential, and the copy read is the redacted one
  prepared for the Assembleia da República and published by a newspaper
  (§1); the IVL annual reports are no-grant publications.  So both are
  CITED, and the figures and short passages used are quoted for commentary
  with their page.  No table or chart is reproduced.
* **Editorial responsibility.**  Research tools were used to locate, extract
  and cross-check the passages; the final editorial validation of every
  statement rests with the publishing author.

## 2. The chain PX → PTA → PET: price, feedstock and spread

| Fact | Source | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| About 0.85 t of PTA is needed per tonne of PET; PTA is about 80 % of the production cost of PET; "El precio de PTA está indexado al precio del paraxileno" (the price of PTA is indexed to the price of paraxylene), so the PET price also moves with the PTA raw materials. | LSB-2012 p. 36 | DOCUMENTED |
| PTA demand depends largely on the textile industry, mainly in Asia (PTA is also the raw material of polyester fibres). | LSB-2012 p. 36 | DOCUMENTED |
| 1 kg of PET resin needs 850 g of PTA and 350 g of MEG (a producer's figure the report quotes); MEG and PTA "historically account for over 75 percent of the cost of producing PET resin". | USITC-4604 p. V-1 | DOCUMENTED |
| PET sales contracts in the US were indexed to raw-material prices. | USITC-4604 p. 20 | DOCUMENTED |
| The "industry spread" IVL publishes is defined as "Selling price less raw material consumption", in US$ per tonne. | IVL-AR2015 PDF p. 98; IVL-AR2016 PDF p. 104; IVL-AR2017 PDF p. 110 | DOCUMENTED |

**What follows for the analysis (CALCULATION / interpretation).**  A PTA
producer earns its revenue by selling PTA.  When the PTA price moves with the
feedstock price, the product price, the feedstock cost and the conversion
cost (energy, catalyst, chemicals, labour, maintenance) have to be analysed
together, and the spread between product price and feedstock cost is the
central measure of the margin; what the plant keeps is that spread less its
own conversion costs.

debt-service reserve 40, guarantee 10, current account 65), after a facility
agreement signed on 22 January 2010 (CPI-2019 pp. 317–318).  LSB-2012 p. 29

10 M and this record does not resolve it.  The November 2008 proposal had CGD
underwrite 100 % pending a syndicate, for which other banks were approached,
and "o sindicato bancário não se veio a materializar" (the bank syndicate did
not materialise) (CPI-2019 p. 309).  While the project finance was being
structured, construction was financed by bridge loans: EUR 117 M by March
2009 and EUR 222 M by January 2010 (CPI-2019 pp. 316–317), five in all
(p. 322).  In August–September 2010 a EUR 55 M capital increase, by new
funds and conversion of debt, reduced LSB to 41 % of Artlant; CGD's venture
fund took 19 % (EUR 17 M), so the lender was also a shareholder (CPI-2019
pp. 318–319).  The inquiry report also records that the financing of
Artlant depended on covenants of LSB's own financings, "facto que veio a
revelar-se prejudicial para o investimento em Sines" (a fact that proved
harmful to the investment in Sines) (CPI-2019 p. 287, ATTRIBUTED).

| LSB's four operating PET plants: 560 000 t/yr nominal, "lo que implica una necesidad de 470.000 toneladas de PTA cada año" (implying a need of 470 000 t of PTA a year). | LSB-2012 p. 37 | operation | DOCUMENTED |

**Arithmetic (CALCULATION).**  At nominal capacity, 700 − 450 = 250 kt/yr
(35.7 %) of the output lies outside the contract's MAXIMUM even with LSB
buying the whole of it; against LSB's whole need, 700 − 470 = 230 kt/yr
(32.9 %).  0.85 × 560 = 476 kt/yr, consistent with LSB's own 470.  In the
page's calculator production in year 2 is 0.9 × 700 = 630 kt (an
ASSUMPTION), so the same 450 kt leave 1 − 450/630 = 28.6 %.

**What this does and does not show.**  The 450 kt/yr is a maximum, not a
guaranteed purchase, and the 35.7 % is the share of nominal capacity not
covered by that maximum — not the whole commercial risk: the volume inside
the maximum was set each year by offer, at a market-indexed price, by a
counterparty whose own credit was a risk.  The absence of explicit
penalties, as LSB-2014 describes the contract, does not show an absence of
other obligations or legal consequences: the same note subjects the contract
to Portuguese commercial law and records a provision booked against
Artlant's claim under it.

**What IVL wrote about the cycle (DOCUMENTED, LATER), quoted:**
* IVL-AR2012 PDF p. 83: "Continuing oversupply of PTA in Asia, which is
  weakening spreads across the value chain in the region and is marginally
  impacting the western markets, in particular the South European markets
  for PET, which seems to have larger appetite for the risk associated with
  imports."
* IVL-AR2013 p. 14: the PTA business "saw massive over-compensation of
  supply following the terrific spike that occurred when cotton prices went
  through the roof in 2010-2011 ... we saw PTA spreads fall to below the
  cash cost of the large majority of the industry."
* IVL-AR2014 PDF p. 21 (an IVL executive, quoted in the report): "The
  tremendous and very rapid expansion of PTA assets occurred mainly in China
  after the high PTA margins of 2011.  An excess of capacity led to depressed
  spreads in Asia while in Europe and North America spreads remained at an
  acceptable level due to their ability to demand fixed cost plus margins
  and with import price parity protection."  The same passage goes on to
  note capacity growth in paraxylene weakening its price and giving PTA
  producers some margin improvement.

**What was recorded AT THE DECISION (DOCUMENTED):** the risk department's
January 2008 opinion, "Previa-se concorrência futura dos mercados
asiáticos" (CPI-2019 p. 303), and its November 2008 opinion, which cites
increasing non-European competition eased by an exchange rate unfavourable
to the euro "conforme referido pelo consultor do projecto" (as the project's
consultant had stated) (CPI-2019 p. 312).  The second passage shows that a
consultant's market view was part of the file.

**What follows (interpretation).**  A regional premium can narrow for
separate reasons, each an input of a scenario: more Asian capacity may
press down the prices at which exports are offered; freight and the
exchange rate change what an import costs.  More capacity is not, in
itself, a reduction of transport or import costs.

**What could not be found:** no open, year-by-year series of Chinese or
Asian PTA capacity for 2008–2016.  The capacity charts inside IVL-AR2013 are
a third party's no-grant data and are not reproduced.

## 10. The page's appraisal: what is documented and what is assumed

The calculator on the page is a SIMPLIFIED, PRE-TAX, UNFINANCED appraisal
for teaching, marked beside its results as "Illustrative teaching scenario —
not an estimate of the historical project's NPV.  Results depend on the
assumptions selected below."  It is not a reconstruction of the business
plan and not a historical valuation; being unfinanced, it cannot conclude
on debt-service capacity.

| Input | Default | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | 700 kt/yr | DOCUMENTED (LSB-2012 p. 29) |
| Capital cost | EUR 425 M; tornado ends 360 and 462.056 | DOCUMENTED (CPI-2019 p. 309: the November 2008 investment, which excludes opex and financing costs); the tornado ends are the lowest and highest stated figures, of different perimeters, not a measure of overrun |
| Captive volume | 450 kt/yr | ASSUMPTION: that LSB buys the contract's maximum every year (the contract set a maximum, LSB-2012; LSB-2014); 700 reproduces the plan's whole-output assumption (CPI-2019 p. 288) |
| Captive customer's need | 470 kt/yr | DOCUMENTED (LSB-2012 p. 37), shown for comparison |
| Spread | 200 US$/t; tornado ends 87 and 231 | ASSUMPTION (no spread available at the decision was found); the tornado ends are retrospective scenarios from published figures of 2014 (IVL-AR2015), Asia and the West index |
| Construction period | 2 years | ASSUMPTION on documented dates (start H1 2008, CPI-2019 p. 303; end planned 2010, CPI-2019 p. 310) |
| Delay | 0; tornado end 2 years | ASSUMPTION (a scenario; PTA came in March 2012, CPI-2019 p. 320); in the model a delay only shifts the operating years and adds no cost |
| First-year utilisation | 60 % | ASSUMPTION (LSB wrote in 2012 that nominal capacity was expected in year 2) |
| Utilisation | 90 % | ASSUMPTION |
| Conversion cost | 60 US$/t | ASSUMPTION |
| Merchant discount | 30 US$/t | ASSUMPTION |
| Fixed costs | EUR 25 M/yr | ASSUMPTION |
| Exchange rate | 1.30 US$/EUR | ASSUMPTION |
| Discount rate | 8 % | ASSUMPTION |
| Life | 15 years | ASSUMPTION (the financing maturity was to be up to 15 years, CPI-2019 pp. 301, 310) |

On these defaults the calculator gives an NPV of about −110 M EUR and a
constant break-even spread of about 232 US$/t (CALCULATION on ASSUMPTIONS):
numbers about the teaching scenario, not about the project.

The arithmetic is a pre-tax discounted cash flow, in
`gui/src/ui/methods/sinesPta.ts`, with closed-form unit tests in
`gui/tests/sinesPta.test.ts`: capital spent in equal parts at the end of
each construction year; operating year k at t = N_c + D + k; cash flow
CF_k = [P_k (S − c_v) − V_m,k d]/X − F; NPV linear in S, so the constant
break-even spread is exact.  NOT modelled, and the page says so: taxes and
depreciation, financing and debt service, working capital, receivables, any
cost of a delay, and a spread that varies over the life.  How taxes and
working capital would move the break-even depends on the tax treatment, on
timing and on the recovery of the working capital; the page does not
presume the direction.  A break-even near a historical average does not
mean the average is needed every year: with a varying spread, the time
sequence of the cash flows decides.

## 12. What was found next to each excerpt

Every transcription, page, translation, date and perimeter was confirmed in
the original documents on 2026-10-24, at an external reviewer's request:
every quotation of §11 was located again in the text extract at the page
given (the multi-column EY and IVL pages word by word), and the passages
around each selected excerpt were read for conditions, mitigants, responses
or conclusions that change its reading.  What they add, and how the page
reads each excerpt as a result:

1. **CPI-2019 p. 313 records a response.**  After the conditions of the
   November 2008 opinion, the report states that the DSF approved the
   conditions and "incorporou o espírito das sugestões da DGR", and that the
   partner condition was not established.  The page quotes both beside the
   conditions (cards 1, 2).
2. **CPI-2019 p. 312 attributes the competition point to the project's
   consultant** ("conforme referido pelo consultor do projecto"), and the
   same opinion notes a possible mitigant (the LSB–Indorama merger under
   negotiation) and that the proposed guarantees were the usual ones for
   that type of financing.  The page quotes the passage in full.
3. **CPI-2019 p. 311: the project-finance terms asked for a pessimistic
   scenario**; EY-final p. 154 records a viability assessment by Caixa BI at
   the initial concession.  Together they show that analyses existed; the
   business plan itself is not public, and the page makes no claim about
   its content.
4. **EY-final p. 154 holds two different statements.**  The project-finance
   sentence is the DGR's, filed by EY under the restructuring, undated; the
   "O projeto de produção assentava ..." sentence is EY's own observation in
   its "other relevant facts" column, and it continues with the reasons EY
   gives (technical questions and the shareholder's insolvency).  The page
   presents them separately (card 6), with the dating left UNCONFIRMED (§9).
5. **EY's "Não" on the accommodation of the DGR's conditions is a finding of
   missing evidence.**  EY-final p. 115 marks "Não"; p. 154 explains that no
   information was made available to verify the accommodation.  The page
   says so and sets it beside CPI-2019 p. 313, which reads the November 2008
   response differently.
6. **LSB-2012 p. 183 and LSB-2014 pp. 122–123 describe the contract as
   mutual obligations on an annual offer, subject to Portuguese law, with a
   provision booked against Artlant's claim.**  The page gives all of it
   (§4), so that "no explicit penalty" is not read as "no obligation".
7. **IVL-AR2014 p. 94: the 163 US$/t is an Asian five-year average** in the
   report for 2014, over a window including the 2010–2011 peak.  The page
   uses it only as a retrospective marker; the calculator's spread is a
   labelled assumption.
8. **EY-final p. 189: the subordination statement is EY's, about CGD's
   claims while it kept and raised its LSB holding**; it does not say by
   whom the classification was made.  The page limits it to this case and
   to EY's framing.
9. **IVL-AR2017 p. 224: the 2017 transaction was an asset purchase,
   through IVL's subsidiary; the fair value is provisional, includes a
   deferred tax liability, and the appraiser's report was not complete.**
   The page gives the bargain-purchase gain as an accounting result, with
   one possible reading among others.
10. **CPI-2019 p. 319 links the exposure to the cost deviations and the

    The page keeps each capital figure with its perimeter and computes no
    ratio between them.
11. **CPI-2019 p. 302: "consolidation not complete"** describes LSB's
    integration of its acquisitions, not its accounts; and the net profit
    of EUR 2.9 M is for 2006.
12. **CPI-2019 pp. 321–322: the later DGR opinions accompanied "algumas"
    (some) of the approvals,** and give their reason (the unpredictability
    of PTA production and sales).
13. **LSB-2014 p. 13: the PER's refinancing agreement was judicially
    approved in 2015.**
