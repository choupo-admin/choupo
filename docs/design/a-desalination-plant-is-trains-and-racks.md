# A desalination plant is trains and racks, not one giant pump and one vessel bank

*2026-11-02, DEV.md 4c C199 (phase A, the engine and the case).  Vítor, after
seeing `swro01_campoDalias`'s layout: in a real installation there is a limit
to the size of the largest pump and the largest pressure exchanger, and the
industry organises the plant by clusters of pumping and pressure recovery.
The case is `tutorials/plant/swro01_campoDalias`, its gate
`bin/curate/check_swro_plant.py`.  Phase B (the GUI: the racks in 3D inside the
hall, a plan and a section) is a later general's.*

## 1. What was wrong with C194's plant

C194 simulated the right plant and organised it wrongly: one `HPpump` of
9.2 MW, one `PX` of 87 rotors, one `RO1` of 1398 vessels, the four pumps sized
as `compressor` items (a pass-through of shaft power), the reverse-osmosis halls
two author-set boxes the pipe router could not reach.  The Toray case study it
is built on says SIX skids of 16 442 m3/d each.

## 2. The decision: simulate once, size per train

Six identical trains in parallel carry six times a train's flow, so the
flowsheet is solved ONCE at plant flow and the sizing is done PER TRAIN.
Alternatives, and why they lost:

* **Six sector copies** of the train (a sectored case, generated by a script):
  honest, but it renames every stream and unit, moves all 48 stream and 414
  kpi rows of the golden, multiplies the Newton problem by six and puts the
  recycle's knife-edge (the case's `0/` is a converged state) six times over.
  The physics does not change; only the organisation does, so the organisation
  is what changes.
* **A `trains N` on every unit**: six homes for one fact.

What was built: ONE declaration, `sizing { trains { count 6; source "..."; reason
"..."; } }` in `system/postDict`, read by `SizingPass` into `result.trains` and
published in the result JSON as `"trains": { count, source, reason }`; and one
switch per sizing entry, `perTrain yes;`. `postProcessing/sizing/TrainSplit.H`
is the one home of the reading (an entry saying `perTrain` with no declared
trains REFUSES by name; one that does not say it is sized as before, byte for
byte) and of the stamp: the sizer returns N items tagged `train1`..`trainN`
(`EquipmentSizing::equipmentTag`, the 1:N mechanism the column uses), each
publishing `trains` and `trainIndex` and, beside its per-train numbers, the
plant's. Identical vessels per train must divide (1398 / 6 = 233, 324 / 6 = 54,
108 / 6 = 18); otherwise the sizer refuses naming the remainder.

## 3. The three sizers

**Pumps: `ratedMachine`, kind `volumeRate`** (`RatedMachineSize.cpp`, no batch
plan read). The load is the pump's own published flow, `F x v_molar_liq`. The
rating, `rating { maxFlowPerUnit; [pressureRating;] origin literature|assumed;
source; reason; }`, is the largest pump of the chosen make and is the case's: no
catalogue is carried, a missing key refuses by name. Published: pumps needed
per train `ceil(Q_train / max)`, `installed { count }` per train (default 1;
fewer than needed REFUSES naming the count), `spare { count }` for the plant,
the pumps running and installed in the plant, the flow and the shaft power per
pump, per train and in the plant, the utilisation (announced above 0.85).

**Pressure exchanger array** (`PressureExchangerSize`): `perTrain`, plus a
maker's `minFlowPerUnit` window and `pressureRating`. The limit is checked in
the SIZER, not the unit: it belongs to the device model the array is built of,
and the unit's physics does not depend on it. A high-pressure inlet or design
pressure above the rating, or a device under the bottom of the window, REFUSES.

**Vessel bank: `membraneBank`** (new, `MembraneBankSize.{H,cpp}`). From the
unit's published `vessels`, `elements`, `elementLength_m`, `elementDiameter_m`
(the last two are new KPIs of `spiralWoundModule`, published only when the
module record declares them) and `area_stage`, plus the case's declared vessel
(`endAllowance`, `outsideDiameter`), rack (`vesselsPerRack`, `tiers`, `pitch`,
`tierPitch`, `baseHeight`, `endMargin`, `aisle`) and `loadingClearance
{ vesselLengths }`:

    vesselLength = elements x elementLength + endAllowance
    racks        = ceil( vesselsPerTrain / vesselsPerRack )
    rackLength   = vesselLength + 2 endMargin
    rackWidth    = (vesselsPerRack / tiers) pitch
    rackHeight   = baseHeight + tiers tierPitch
    loadingClearance = k vesselLength               (free, at the +x end)
    bank = racks side by side with an aisle between them
    envelopeLength = rackLength + loadingClearance
    envelopeWidth  = racks rackWidth + (racks - 1) aisle
    envelopeHeight = rackHeight

The envelope is published as `envelopeLength/Width/Height` in metres, and
`PlantLayout.cpp::publishedFootprint` reads it as a BOX beside the cylinder
pairs it already read: no footprint grammar was added to the dict. A pitch
under the vessel diameter, a vessel narrower than its element, a rack that does
not divide into tiers and any missing declared number refuse by name.

## 4. The RO hall is a building

`system/layoutDict` declares ONE `building {}` (C100) whose shell `roBuilding`
(151 x 63 x 10 m) holds four rooms, each listing the items that stand in it
(their base is the room floor; the engine refuses an item that does not fit
its room or stands in a room that does not list it): `pumpHall` (1845 m2, the
18 pumps of the six trains), `ro1Hall` (4182 m2, the six RO1 banks), `ro2Hall`
(1722 m2, the 12 second-pass banks) and `pxHall` (952 m2, the six arrays).
The GMP grammar's required fields are filled honestly: every room is grade CNC
at the outside pressure, so the cascade has nothing to say and says so in its
`source`. Pipes with one end indoors are not routed (the engine's rule); the
three lines that leave the building are DECLARED from their wall penetration
(`pipes {}`), which needed the fence `inset` set to the 5 m plot margin so that
a declared route can end ON the fence. The router draws one line per stream, to
the first train's item; the six lines are identical.

## 6. What was read, and what was not

Read (open pages, 2026-11-02, by this general): Sulzer MSD-RO brochure e10110
(PDF, in full) and product page (train capacities 20 000-30 000 m3/d; 1000-1600
m3/h; up to 90 bar); Energy Recovery PX Q-series page (4.5-147.6 m3/h, 20-650
gpm; up to 83 bar) and the PX Q650 press release of 9 March 2026 (250-650 gpm);
CompositesWorld, composites' role in SWRO (Carlsbad: 2236 vessels, eight
elements each, 1200/600/450 psi; and 8-inch, 28.4-foot vessels at the SANTA
BARBARA plant). NOT read: Danfoss's page answered 403 (only a search summary was
seen, so it is context, not a number); Hydranautics TSB122 and LG Chem TSB 102
were search results only; **no open source gave a loading clearance, a rack
pitch, an aisle or a rack size**, so those are declared `assumed`. The C199
note that put the 28.4 ft vessel at Carlsbad is wrong (the page says Santa
Barbara); the vessel end allowance (28.4 ft less eight 40-inch elements =
0.53 m) is therefore derived from another plant's vessel and assumed to hold.
"Carlsbad runs three pumps and a spare" was in the commission and was not
re-read; the spares are declared `assumed`.
