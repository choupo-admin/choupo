# One record, two vapours: acetic acid's latent heat

*2026-10-11, DEV.md 4c C65 item (a).  A finding of the C60 safety review,
measured again here against the primaries, with the readers of the field
enumerated and the obvious data fix's blast radius MEASURED.  Nothing in the
catalogue was changed: no honest fix fits inside a record, and the one that
does fit is an engine decision reserved for Vítor.*

## 1. What the record says, and what each half of it means

`data/standards/components/aceticAcid.dat` carries ONE formation datum, as
the rule says (`check_record_form`: one datum per component, the other
phases derive):

    standardThermochemistry { dHf_298 -432250.0; s_298 282.500; }   // ideal gas

and a latent heat

    Tb      391.05;
    HvapTb  24390;

The two halves speak about DIFFERENT vapours.

* **The datum is the ideal-gas MONOMER.**  -432.25 kJ/mol is
  -103.31 kcal/mol, NBS Technical Note 270-3 (Wagman, Evans, Parker, Halow,
  Bailey & Schumm, 1968), Table 23, "CH3COOH acetic acid, g" -- read on the
  page image, p. 133 of the report; a US-government work.  Chao & Zwolinski,
  J. Phys. Chem. Ref. Data 7, 363 (1978) (read, srd.nist.gov reprint) adopt
  that same value for the MONOMER, `ΔHf(CH3COOH, g, 298.15 K) = -103.31
  kcal mol-1`, and build their dimer and equilibrium-mixture tables on it.
  The same NBS page gives the LIQUID: -115.8 kcal/mol = **-484.5 kJ/mol**
  (S 38.2 cal/K mol), and the gas entropy 67.5 cal/K mol = 282.4 J/K mol,
  which is the record's `s_298 282.500`.
* **The latent heat is to the ASSOCIATED vapour.**  24.39 kJ/mol at the
  normal boiling point is the heat a calorimeter measures when liquid acetic
  acid becomes its real, equilibrium vapour -- in which a large share of the
  molecules are hydrogen-bonded dimers.  Forming those dimers releases heat,
  so the measured latent heat is far smaller than the heat needed to make the
  monomer gas the datum describes.

## 2. What the engine derives from the pair

`Component::h_formation` (`src/thermo/Component.cpp:1133-1134`, the
cross-phase leg) prices the liquid as

    h_liq(T) = dHf_298(gas) - Hvap_Watson(298.15 K) + ∫ Cp_liq dT

Watson from (Tb 391.05 K, HvapTb 24390 J/mol, Tc 591.95 K) gives
Hvap(298.15 K) = 24390 x [(1 - 298.15/591.95)/(1 - 391.05/591.95)]^0.38
= **28.18 kJ/mol**, so the engine's liquid acetic acid sits at
**-460.43 kJ/mol** against the NBS liquid's **-484.5**: 24.07 kJ/mol too
high.  The monomer-basis vaporisation enthalpy the two NBS entries imply is
-432.25 - (-484.5) = **52.25 kJ/mol** at 298.15 K.

**The tree's own records explain the difference, to within 2 kJ/mol.**
`data/standards/chemistry/aceticAcid-dissolution.dat` `vapourDimerisation`
carries Chao & Zwolinski's dimerisation, log K25 = 2.555 (bar^-1) and
dH = -63.18 kJ per mol of dimer.  At 298.15 K the record's Antoine gives
Psat = 0.02113 bar; solving K = p_d / p_m^2 with p_m + p_d = Psat gives
p_m = 0.00640 bar, p_d = 0.01473 bar, so **82.1 %** of the acid molecules in
the saturated vapour are dimerised and 0.4106 mol of dimer forms per mole
vaporised, releasing 0.4106 x 63.18 = **25.94 kJ/mol**.  Monomer basis
= 28.18 + 25.94 = 54.12 kJ/mol against NBS's 52.25 -- the residual 1.9 kJ/mol
is Watson's extrapolation of an associating latent heat from 391 K to 298 K,
which is not a correlation's home ground.  The C60 review reproduced the same
25.9 kJ/mol on the EduTool page.

## 3. Who reads the field (enumerated, `grep` over `src/`, 2026-10-11)

Every reader goes through `Component::Hvap_latent(T)` (Watson from
`HvapTb`) or the raw `Hvap_Tb_`:

| Reader | What it uses the number for | Which basis it NEEDS |
|---|---|---|
| `Component::h_formation` gas<->liquid leg | the liquid formation rung, every liquid enthalpy on the formation surface | MONOMER (the datum's) |
| `ThermoPackage::Hliquid`/`Hvapour`/`Hmixture` (the Tref surface; called from `Mixer`, `DistillationColumn`, `Pump`, `Heater`, `MultiStreamHX`, `AdiabaticFlash`, `IsothermalFlash`) | latent heat between a liquid and an IDEAL-GAS vapour | whichever vapour the unit models -- ASSOCIATED if the vapour is ideal-monomer and the duty is to mean a real heat |
| `propertyOps/SolubilityParameter` (+ `check_record_self_consistency`, anchor 19060 Pa^0.5) | Hildebrand delta = sqrt((Hvap(298) - RT)/Vliq) | the tabulated delta uses the APPARENT (measured) heat |
| `propertyOps/PropertyEvaluator` (`Hvap` key), `HConsistency` | prints / checks the latent heat | the measured one |
| `ReliefValveSize` (`ReliefValveSize.cpp:483`, the mixture latent heat at the bubble point) | vapour generated by a fire duty | the real (associated) vapour |
| ebullioscopic `K_b` derivation (opt-in) | R Tb^2 M / Hvap | the measured one at Tb |
| the evaporator / spray dryer / psychrometric Watson fallbacks | solvent latent heat when no enthalpy datum exists | (water in the corpus; acetic acid reaches none of them) |

So the field has readers that need BOTH bases.  That is the finding: the
record is not wrong in one number -- it answers two questions with one
number, and the correct answer differs by ~26 kJ/mol.

## 4. The fix that fits in the record, and why it was not taken

**Option A: restate `HvapTb` on the monomer basis**, back-calculated so that
Watson(298.15 K) = 52.25 kJ/mol: `HvapTb 45222`.  The liquid rung then lands
on NBS's -484.5 kJ/mol to the joule, and the C60 liquid heat of reaction
(-25.95 kJ/mol at 330 K, against ~-3.6 measured) collapses to about zero.

It was MEASURED, not argued: every one of the 38 corpus cases that declares
acetic acid (all 38 are sealed, so the catalogue edit alone moves nothing)
was copied to a scratch directory, its mirror edited exactly as a re-import
would leave it (both manifest hashes recomputed), and run against its
golden; an unedited control copy of each was run the same way and passed
38/38.  Result in §4.1: **34 of 38 cases fail, 400 golden rows move.**

And it breaks three other readers by construction:

* **every acetic-acid VAPORISATION on an ideal-monomer vapour doubles**:
  a column, a flash or an evaporator that boils acetic acid with no dimer in
  its vapour model would charge 45 kJ/mol at Tb where a calorimeter measures
  24 -- the latent heat would be right for a gas that does not exist;
* the **Hildebrand parameter** derived from the record goes from about
  21.2 to 29.4 (MPa)^0.5 against the 19.06 the record anchors, so
  `check_record_self_consistency` would fail on a correct derivation of a
  wrong input;
* the **relief-valve fire case** would size for half the vapour a fire
  actually generates.

A data fix that makes one reader right by making three wrong is the arity sin
pointed the other way.

## 5. The fix that is honest, and why it is the engine's, not the record's

The physics is unambiguous: acetic acid's vapour is associated, and an
ideal-MONOMER vapour cannot be priced against a correct liquid without
pricing the association.  The record should keep BOTH facts it already
has -- the measured latent heat (to the associated vapour) and the monomer
datum -- and SAY which vapour `HvapTb` speaks; the engine should then
derive the monomer-basis crossing from the record's own dimerisation
equilibrium (the §2 arithmetic, which reproduces NBS to 1.9 kJ/mol) and
price the vapour's association wherever the vapour is acetic acid's, not
only inside a reactive flash.  Two pieces, both architecture:

1. **The liquid rung reads the association** (a chemistry record, today
   reached only by the reactive VLE builder) -- a component's enthalpy would
   then depend on a record outside the component, a layering decision.
2. **Every vapour carrying acetic acid prices its dimer** (the chemical
   theory of vapour imperfection: the `flash12`/`flash13` treatment, which
   already re-weights v = t_mono + 2 t_dim and prices the association heat,
   made the default for associating vapours) -- otherwise fixing (1) alone
   produces Option A's doubled latent heat on every non-reactive case.

Doing (1) without (2) is Option A by another route.  Doing (2) moves every
acetic-acid case with a vapour.  RESERVED for Vítor: which vapour the
record's `HvapTb` speaks (the measured one, on this record's reading), and
whether association becomes a property of the vapour model rather than of
one flash formulation.  Until then the C60 EduTool page carries the
finding as a limitation, reproduced from the records by a test, which is
the honest state.

## 6. A latent double count worth knowing about

`flash12`/`flash13` price the dimer association heat INTO the duty on top
of a liquid derived from the apparent `HvapTb`, which already contains it.
Measured on flash13 when the dimer record was adopted (2026-08-24, the
record's own comment): the association term is -0.25 kW against a 665.8 kW
duty, so at that case's vacuum and temperature the double count is small.
It is not small at 298 K and 1 atm, where 82 % of the vapour is dimer.
