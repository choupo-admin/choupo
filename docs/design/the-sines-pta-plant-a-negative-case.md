## 2. The chain PX → PTA → PET, and why the margin is a spread

| Fact | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| About 0.85 t of PTA is needed per tonne of PET; PTA is about 80 % of the production cost of PET; "El precio de PTA está indexado al precio del paraxileno" (the price of PTA is indexed to the price of paraxylene), so the PET price also moves with the PTA raw materials. | LSB-2012 p. 36 | P |
| 1 kg of PET resin needs 850 g of PTA and 350 g of MEG (a producer's figure the report quotes); MEG and PTA "historically account for over 75 percent of the cost of producing PET resin". | USITC-4604 p. V-1 | P |
| PET sales contracts in the US were indexed to raw-material prices. | USITC-4604 p. 20 | P |
| The "industry spread" IVL publishes is defined as "Selling price less raw material consumption", in US$ per tonne. | IVL-AR2015 PDF p. 98; IVL-AR2016 PDF p. 104; IVL-AR2017 PDF p. 110 | P |

**What follows for the analysis (A).**  When the product price is a formula
on the feedstock price, a PTA producer is not paid "the PTA price"; it is
paid the SPREAD between the two, and what it keeps is that spread minus its
conversion cost (energy, catalyst, chemicals, labour, maintenance).  A plan
must therefore be appraised on the spread, and on the spread's history and
range, never on a product price.

## 4. The offtake: plan, contract and need

| Fact | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| The investment assumed contracts for the purchase of the WHOLE output by LSB for its PET production ("a celebração de contratos de compra da totalidade produção da ARTLANT"). | CPI-2019 p. 288 | P |
| The risk department wrote in November 2008 that sales depended on LSB honouring an offtake contract "(não assegurado)" (not secured) for 100 % of the output. | CPI-2019 p. 312 | P |
| The project-finance terms of November 2008 included an LSB purchase contract at a price allowing a minimum EBITDA, and "Cenário pessimista que assegurasse o serviço da dívida suportado apenas em vendas à LSB" (a pessimistic scenario ensuring debt service supported only by sales to LSB). | CPI-2019 p. 311 | P |
| The contract signed (dated 15 September 2010 in LSB-2012, August 2010 in LSB-2014): supply of UP TO 450 000 t/yr, at variable prices linked to a market reference index, from the month after start-up to 2030. | LSB-2012 pp. 29, 37; LSB-2014 p. 122 | P |
| "El citado contrato no establece explícitamente penalizaciones en caso de que el Grupo ... adquiera una cantidad inferior a la acordada en la Oferta Anual" (the contract does not explicitly set penalties if the group buys less than agreed in the annual offer). | LSB-2014 pp. 122–123 | P |
| LSB's four operating PET plants: 560 000 t/yr nominal, "lo que implica una necesidad de 470.000 toneladas de PTA cada año" (implying a need of 470 000 t of PTA a year). | LSB-2012 p. 37 | P |

Arithmetic (A): at nominal capacity, 700 − 450 = 250 kt/yr (35.7 %) had to
be sold to third parties even with LSB buying its whole contracted maximum;
against LSB's whole need, 700 − 470 = 230 kt/yr (32.9 %).  0.85 × 560 = 476
kt/yr, consistent with LSB's own 470.  The plan's "whole output to the
shareholder" was therefore not reachable even on paper: about a third of the
plant was a MERCHANT plant from the first day, and the contract for the
other two thirds carried no explicit volume penalty and a market-indexed
price.

**What IVL wrote about the cycle (P), quoted, persons not named:**
* IVL-AR2012 PDF p. 83: "Continuing oversupply of PTA in Asia, which is
  weakening spreads across the value chain in the region and is marginally
  impacting the western markets, in particular the South European markets
  for PET, which seems to have larger appetite for the risk associated with
  imports."
* IVL-AR2013 p. 14: the PTA business "saw massive over-compensation of
  supply following the terrific spike that occurred when cotton prices went
  through the roof in 2010-2011 ... we saw PTA spreads fall to below the
  cash cost of the large majority of the industry."
* IVL-AR2014 PDF p. 21: "The tremendous and very rapid expansion of PTA
  assets occurred mainly in China after the high PTA margins of 2011.  An
  excess of capacity led to depressed spreads in Asia while in Europe and
  North America spreads remained at an acceptable level due to their ability
  to demand fixed cost plus margins and with import price parity
  protection."

**What follows (A).**  The Western spread is held up by IMPORT PARITY: it
stays high only while importing from Asia costs more.  Anything that cheapens
imports — new Asian capacity, freight, an exchange rate — moves the Western
spread toward the Asian one.  A European merchant plant is exposed to
exactly that.

**What could not be found:** no open, year-by-year series of Chinese or
Asian PTA capacity for 2008–2016.  The capacity charts inside IVL-AR2013 are
a third party's no-grant data and are not reproduced.

CGD's risk department (Direção de Gestão de Risco, DGR) wrote opinions that
are documented in CPI-2019 and EY-final.  They are listed as the analysis
should read them: each warning, and the concept of process economics that
catches it.  All **P** unless marked.

| # | Warning, as documented | Source | The concept that catches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales depended on an offtake contract "(não assegurado)" for 100 % of the output; the conditions asked for "contratos firmes de off take, a preços fixos" (firm offtake contracts at fixed prices). | CPI-2019 pp. 312–313 | Offtake security and customer concentration: a plan is appraised on the volume that is contracted, and the rest is a merchant share priced at the market. |
| 2 | "Risco de incumprimento da LSB" (risk of LSB default); "o desconhecimento da 'situação real de risco e de mercado' da LSB" (lack of knowledge of LSB's real risk and market situation); in January 2008, LSB's consolidated accounts were incomplete and hard to assess, net profit EUR 2.9 M on EUR 645 M of sales, financial debt EUR 605 M. | CPI-2019 pp. 302–303, 312 | Single-customer credit risk: the captive customer's own balance sheet is an input of the appraisal; a downside case in which it stops buying (and paying). |
| 3 | Paraxylene "era uma commodity ... com uma volatilidade elevada do preço" (was a commodity with high price volatility). | CPI-2019 p. 312 | Feedstock security: a supply contract and the working capital to buy feedstock; the margin is a spread, so the analysis is of the spread, not of either price. |
| 4 | "Previa-se concorrência futura dos mercados asiáticos" (future competition from Asian markets was foreseen, Jan 2008); "aumento da concorrência extra-europeia [...] onde uma taxa de câmbio desfavorável ao euro facilita a entrada de concorrentes asiáticos" (increase of non-European competition, where an exchange rate unfavourable to the euro eases the entry of Asian competitors, Nov 2008). | CPI-2019 pp. 303, 312 | Market capacity additions and import parity: the Western spread is set by what an import costs, so the analysis needs the capacity pipeline and the exchange rate as inputs. |
| 5 | "volatilidade do preço das matérias primas" (raw-material price volatility) and sector consolidation favouring larger operators; the condition of a bank guarantee covering the difference between actual EBITDA and the minimum EBITDA for debt service. | CPI-2019 pp. 303, 313 | Volatility and downside cases: the break-even spread set beside the spreads actually observed, and a stated pessimistic case. |
| 6 | The premises "(ausência de contratos com entidades externas ao Grupo que permitissem assegurar a estabilidade de cash flows para suportar o serviço da dívida) não se enquadram com uma estrutura de project finance" (the absence of contracts with entities outside the group able to secure stable cash flows for debt service does not fit a project-finance structure); "O projeto de produção assentava essencialmente no escoamento da produção para o principal acionista" (the production project rested essentially on selling the output to the main shareholder). | EY-final p. 154 | Project finance repays debt from the project's own cash flows, so it needs contracts with third parties; a related party's promise is the sponsor's risk again. |
| 7 | CGD to keep at most one third of the financing after syndication; the syndicate did not materialise and CGD "viu-se forçada a deter a totalidade do projeto, sendo, no fim, o seu único financiador" (found itself forced to hold the whole project, being, in the end, its sole financier); by keeping and raising its holding in LSB while lending to it and to a subsidiary, CGD "foi considerada um credor subordinado" (was considered a subordinated creditor); CGD's fund also held 19 % of Artlant. | CPI-2019 pp. 309, 313, 318, 323; EY-final p. 189 | Concentration of the lender's exposure, and the conflict of a lender that becomes a shareholder: its claim ranks behind other creditors when the borrower fails. |
| 8 | Five bridge loans were made before the project finance was contracted in 2010: "permitiu-se o avanço da construção da fábrica enquanto decorriam as due diligences necessárias" (construction was allowed to advance while the necessary due diligences ran); during them, soil contamination was detected; the mortgage on the surface right lost value "devido a problemas ambientais" (owing to environmental problems). | CPI-2019 pp. 314, 322; EY-final p. 154 | Stage gates: capital is committed after the due diligence of the gate, not during it (the Front-End Loading page). |
