\section*{What this document is}

Every number and every model name below was read off the case's own files or
off a Choupo run of it. Nothing here is a summary written from memory: each
section names the file the fact lives in, so you can open it and disagree.

That matters because this case runs \textbf{three different thermodynamic
worlds at once}, and a stream that crosses between them is re-priced on the
other side. If you do not know which world a unit computes in, you cannot read
its energy balance.

\section{Three property packages, one per world}

A Choupo case declares its thermophysical system in
\texttt{constant/thermoPhysPropDict}, and in a sectored case that file exists at
every level: a sector's own declaration \emph{replaces} the plant's for the
units inside it.

\toprule
\textbf{Where} & \textbf{Solvent} & \textbf{Solutes (Henry)} & \textbf{Why} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\texttt{constant/} (plant) & NH$_3$, pure-liquid rung &
  N$_2$, H$_2$, Ar &
  The loop and the product: the condensed phase is liquid ammonia. \\
\texttt{PURIFICATION/\allowbreak{}constant/} & water, pure-liquid rung &

  The only liquid here is the condensate the deoxo reaction makes. \\
\texttt{RECOVERY/\allowbreak{}constant/} & water, pure-liquid rung &
  NH$_3$, N$_2$, H$_2$, Ar, O$_2$ &
  The scrubber dissolves ammonia into water. \\
\bottomrule

All three declare \texttt{formulation diluteSolution}, all three carry the same
six components (\texttt{N2 H2 NH3 Ar water O2}), and all three price the vapour
with \textbf{SRK} (\texttt{vapour \{ fugacityModel SRK; \}}). The liquid is
where they differ.

\subsection{What \texttt{diluteSolution} means here}

Two reference rungs in one liquid phase, chosen per component and declared:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item the \textbf{solvent} sits on the \texttt{pureLiquid} rung --- the Raoult
      reference, activity $\to 1$ as $x \to 1$;
\item every \textbf{solute} sits on the \texttt{infiniteDilution} rung ---
      the Henry reference, with \texttt{solutionModel henryDilute}.
\end{itemize}

This is a \emph{declared} choice with a stated domain of validity, not a
default. It holds while the solutes stay dilute. The \texttt{RECOVERY} package
says so in its own header: when the scrubber liquid reaches roughly 15\,\%
ammonia by mass the speciation starts to matter, Henry stops holding, and the
package has to change. \textbf{The records for that are already in the case}
--- \texttt{NH3.dat} declares \texttt{aqueousSpeciation ammonia;} as an
identity fact, which pulls \texttt{chemistry/NH3aq-formation.dat},
\texttt{chemistry/water-dissociation.dat} and the species NH$_4^+$, OH$^-$,
H$^+$ into the case --- so switching formulation is a declaration change, not a
data hunt.

\subsection{Model boundaries, and what they do to enthalpy}

Two streams cross between worlds: \texttt{Makeup} (\texttt{PURIFICATION}
$\to$ \texttt{LOOP}) and \texttt{Purge} (\texttt{LOOP} $\to$
\texttt{RECOVERY}).

\begin{caveat}
At a model boundary Choupo holds $(T, P, z)$ and lets each side compute its own
enthalpy. \textbf{$H$ is the conserved truth; $T$ is the model-dependent
readout.} The step you see in the first-law ledger at those two streams is two
models disagreeing about $H(T,P,z)$ --- it is information, not a solver
failure, and Choupo prints it rather than hiding it in a silent temperature
nudge.
\end{caveat}

\section{The vapour phase: SRK}

Soave--Redlich--Kwong, one equation of state for the vapour in all three
packages, with $k_{ij} = 0$ (announced by the run). It needs three constants
per component, and this case's records declare them as follows.

Every component also declares an \textbf{Antoine} vapour-pressure correlation
and a \textbf{polynomial} ideal-gas heat capacity, each with its own validity
window. Choupo announces every extrapolation outside a declared window at the
end of the run --- the base case reports \measured{32} of them --- so the
caveat block is where you find out whether a correlation was used where its
author intended.

\begin{caveat}
Hydrogen at $\omega = -0.220$ and a critical temperature of 33\,K is being
priced by a cubic equation of state at 200\,bar and 840\,K. That is a long way
outside where SRK was fitted, and it is a legitimate thing to say in your
report. Choupo does not stop you; it tells you.
\end{caveat}

stores them as records under \texttt{parameters/Henry/}, each declaring its
convention (\texttt{Sander-Hxp-v1}), its model (\texttt{vantHoff}), its
reference fugacity, its enthalpy of dissolution, its partial molar volume where

The three ammonia-solvent pairs also carry a Krichevsky--Kasarnovsky Poynting
volume, $v_\infty \approx 2.3$--$2.4\times10^{-5}$\,m$^3$/mol, and a Margules
$A = 0$ (the dilute-ideal activity holds across the data). \textbf{The sign of
$\Delta H_{\mathrm{diss}}$ flips between the two solvents}: the permanent gases
are \emph{more} soluble in hot ammonia and \emph{less} soluble in hot water.
That is not a typing error, and a student who notices it has understood
something.

\subsection{The one pair this project had to fit: Ar--NH$_3$}

The catalogue had no argon-in-ammonia pair, and \textbf{the absence was not
neutral}. With no Henry pair, argon falls onto the Raoult rung, and its
``vapour pressure'' at the 250\,K separator then comes from an Antoine
extrapolation \emph{above its own critical temperature}
($T_c = 150.86$\,K). That gave $K_{\mathrm{Ar}} = 1.7$ against
$K_{\mathrm{N_2}} = 124$ --- argon roughly seventy times more soluble in liquid
ammonia than nitrogen --- so 88\,\% of the argon left dissolved in the product
instead of accumulating in the loop, and the purge, which is the heart of this
case, was left with no physical meaning. Exit code 0 throughout.

\begin{caveat}
\textbf{This is the most useful single lesson in the case.} A missing
parameter did not produce an error. It produced a plausible number, from a
correlation used far outside the window its author declared, and it quietly
removed the phenomenon the whole flowsheet exists to manage.
\end{caveat}

The pair was therefore fitted for this project, and the whole derivation is
reproducible: \texttt{curation/Ar-NH3/fit-Ar-NH3.py}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Primary data}, all three sets classified TENTATIVE by
      C.\,L.\ Young's critical evaluation in IUPAC--NIST Solubility Data
      Series Vol.~4 (\emph{Argon}), H.\,L.\ Clever (ed.), 1980, pp.~314--318:
      Kaminishi, G., \emph{Kogyo Kagaku Zasshi} \textbf{68} (1965) 419
      [\emph{Int.\ Chem.\ Eng.}\ \textbf{5} (1965) 749, English translation]
      --- $x_{\mathrm{Ar}}$ at 273.15 / 298.15 / 323.15\,K, 50.8 to
      199.9\,bar, the anchor set because it covers exactly the loop pressure;
      Matou\v{s}, Sobr \& Nov\'ak, \emph{Coll.\ Czech.\ Chem.\ Comm.}\
      \textbf{35} (1970) 3757 (243--303\,K --- the only set reaching below
      273\,K, i.e.\ covering the 250\,K separator); and Michels, Dumoulin
      \& Van Dijk, \emph{Physica} \textbf{27} (1961) 886.
\item \textbf{A correction was applied, and it is declared rather than
      silent.} As printed in the compilation, the Matou\v{s} values are ten
      times Kaminishi's at the single overlapping point, which contradicts the
      critical evaluation's own statement that the studies agree. Read as
      $10^{1}x_{\mathrm{Ar}}$ they fall within $\pm4$\,\% of the van 't Hoff
      line at every temperature. That reading is adopted and marked \emph{to be
      confirmed against the original 1970 paper}.
\item \textbf{Independent validation, held out of the fit.} Michels 1961 ---
      a different laboratory, a different method, with $y_{\mathrm{Ar}}$
      measured --- gives $H = 4.56\times10^{8}$\,Pa, $-6$\,\% from the adopted
      value.
\item \textbf{Family consistency.} $v_\infty = 23$\,cm$^3$/mol against 23
      (N$_2$) and 24 (H$_2$) in the same solvent; argon is 1.8 times more
      soluble than nitrogen in liquid ammonia, against 2.2 times in water.
      The same order of magnitude, not seventy times.
\end{itemize}

This pair is the case's one piece of original curation, and it is the model for
what Choupo asks of any parameter you add: primary sources, a stated
correction, a held-out validation set, a declared window, and a script that
reproduces the fit.

\section{The reaction: the deoxo bed}

Declared in \texttt{PURIFICATION/constant/reactions}:
\[
  \mathrm{O_2} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2} \;\longrightarrow\; 2\,\mathrm{H_2O}
\]
with \texttt{limitingReactant O2} and \texttt{conversion 1.0} in
\texttt{PURIFICATION/Deoxo} --- hydrogen is in roughly 700-fold excess, so
complete conversion of the oxygen is the real specification of a deoxo bed
(outlet O$_2$ below 1\,ppm).

\begin{caveat}
\textbf{The heat of reaction is not declared anywhere and must not be.} Choupo
computes it as $\Delta H_{\mathrm{rxn}}(T) = \sum_i \nu_i h_i(T)$ from each
species' own formation enthalpy in its \texttt{standardThermochemistry} block.
A \texttt{dH\_rxn} key in a reactions dict is an announced, cross-checked
\emph{override} for toy systems that lack formation data --- never a primary
input. One enthalpy datum, one place.
\end{caveat}

Measured on the base case: \measured{2.594\,kmol/h} of oxygen removed, releasing
\measured{348.7\,kW}, and making \measured{81.05\,kg/h} of water --- which is
the entire reason the chiller, the knock-out drum and the dryer exist
downstream.

\section{The converter: Gibbs equilibrium, not kinetics}

\texttt{LOOP/Converter} is a \texttt{gibbsReactor} in \texttt{mode adiabatic}
at 200\,bar. It minimises Gibbs energy over the declared species
(N$_2$, H$_2$, NH$_3$, Ar) subject to the declared element balances
(N, H, Ar), and finds the outlet temperature that closes the energy balance.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{There is no rate law and no catalyst in this model.} The result
      is the equilibrium the bed would reach given infinite contact time. A
      real converter approaches it; how closely is a catalyst question this
      case does not ask.
\item Argon is carried as an inert species: it takes part in no reaction but
      occupies volume and dilutes the reactants, which is exactly its role in
      the real loop.
\item Measured: outlet at \measured{839.0\,K} carrying
      \measured{11.01\,mol\,\%} ammonia.
\end{itemize}

\begin{caveat}
\textbf{An open item in Choupo, stated here rather than discovered by you.}
The adiabatic Gibbs reactor solves its energy balance on the ideal-gas
enthalpy surface while its streams are priced with the SRK departure. At
200\,bar those are not the same surface, and the difference lands in the
first-law ledger. This is a known engine-level issue, not a defect in your
case.
\end{caveat}

\section{The dryer: adsorption equilibrium}

\texttt{PURIFICATION/Dryer} is a \texttt{tsaTwinBed}: 1500\,kg of 4A molecular
sieve per column, 12\,h of adsorption per bed, regenerated at 553\,K with
0.5\,\% of the product gas as purge. Its equilibrium comes from
\texttt{PURIFICATION/constant/parameters/adsorption/equilibria/molSieve4A}.

The adsorbent's bulk density (700\,kg/m$^3$) and solid heat capacity
(920\,J/kg\,K) are declared in \texttt{PURIFICATION/constant/adsorbents/} and
\textbf{marked, in the record itself, as a case-local teaching anchor rather
than curated data}: representative material-class values for a commercial 4A
sieve in 3\,mm beads, not design data, and explicitly not to be promoted to the
standards catalogue. Measured: the bed runs at \measured{52.7\,\%} of its
working capacity, which is a design margin you may argue with.

size never looks like a computed one.

USD/EUR 0.92, contingency and fee $1.18\times C_{\mathrm{BM}}$, site factor
0.50. Economics: discounted cash flow, 20-year life, 8\,\% discount rate,
21\,\% tax, straight-line depreciation over 10 years, working capital 15\,\% of
FCI, stream factor 0.913.

\subsection{What the run says about its own sizing}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The gas volumetric flow in the vessel sizer is $NRT/P$}, ideal
      gas --- printed in the sizer's own basis line, next to the number.
      At 200\,bar in the loop this is the one place in the chain where the
      stated basis and the case's thermodynamic package disagree.
\item \textbf{SS316 is rated 100\,bar} in \texttt{constant/assets/SS316.dat}
      and the Converter and Separator are designed at 240\,bar. Warned per
      vessel, priced anyway.
\item \textbf{\texttt{PRODUCT.Letdown} at $V_R = 0.125$\,m$^3$} falls below the
      Turton correlation's range $[0.3, 520]$; its cost is extrapolated and
      says so.
\end{itemize}
