\section*{Why this edition exists}

The first edition of this brief asked you to appraise the plant with hydrogen
bought at 4.00\,EUR/kg and ammonia sold at 0.60\,EUR/kg. Run that pair through
the case and the answer is a net present value of
\measured{$-680$ million EUR}: the plant loses more than its entire fixed
capital investment every year, and no decision you can take inside the battery
limits changes that: the Converter is the most expensive item in the plant and
its whole total-module cost is \measured{8.4 million EUR}, against a hole of
680.

That is not a defect of the flowsheet. It is arithmetic, and it is worth seeing
in one line. The case consumes \measured{181.1\,kg of hydrogen per tonne of
ammonia} (measured; the stoichiometric minimum is 177.6, the extra 2.0\,\% is
the loop purge). At 4.00\,EUR/kg that is \measured{725\,EUR of hydrogen inside
each tonne of ammonia}, sold for 600. You lose 0.12\,EUR on every kilogram
before touching capital, utilities, labour or catalyst.

The lesson is real and it is the most important thing this project can teach
you --- \textbf{but it is a lesson about a price pair, not about a design}, and
a brief that hides it behind a fixed price list teaches the wrong reflex. This
edition therefore makes the hydrogen price an \emph{object of the study}
instead of a given, and asks you to defend the number you choose.

\section{What you are designing --- and what you are not}

\begin{caveat}
\textbf{Battery limits.} The electrolyser park and the air separation unit are
\textbf{outside}. Hydrogen and nitrogen arrive at the fence as purchased raw
materials. Everything this case sizes, costs and appraises is the
\textbf{synthesis loop plus its compression and purification}.
\end{caveat}

This matters more than it looks. The fixed capital investment the case computes
is \measured{29.9 million EUR}; the raw-material bill at the first edition's
prices is \measured{129.3 million EUR per year}. The capital you are designing
is worth about three months of the hydrogen you are buying. \textbf{The
investment decision for a green ammonia plant lives in the electricity-to-hydrogen
step, which is outside your fence} --- and one of the deliverables below is to
show that quantitatively rather than to be told it.

\midrule

\textbf{The nitrogen is the interesting declaration.} Analytical-grade nitrogen
at 99.999\,\% is what you buy in a cylinder to feed a chromatograph. This plant
consumes 17\,t/h of it: at that rate you take what the air separation column
gives you, which is 99.5 to 99.9\,\%, with oxygen as the dominant impurity.
Three units in \texttt{PURIFICATION} exist only because of that one line, and
the argon in it builds up in the loop to \measured{1.38\,mol\,\%} at the
converter --- which is what sets the purge, which is what sets the hydrogen
you throw away.

\section{The economics --- and the price you have to defend}

\subsection{Two ways to get hydrogen, and they are not the same question}

\textbf{Scenario M (merchant).} You buy hydrogen over the fence at a quoted
price $p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$ [EUR/kg]. This is the case as it ships, and it is the
right model for a plant that sits next to somebody else's electrolyser.

\textbf{Scenario I (integrated).} The project owns the electrolysers. There is
then no market price --- there is an electricity price and a conversion
efficiency:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{H_2}} \;=\; \frac{e \, p_e}{1000} \;+\; c_{\mathrm{el}},
  \label{eq:pH2}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the specific consumption of the electrolyser [kWh per kg\,H$_2$],
$p_e$ the delivered electricity price [EUR/MWh] and $c_{\mathrm{el}}$ the
share of the electrolyser's own capital and maintenance carried by each
kilogram [EUR/kg]. \textbf{Both $e$ and $c_{\mathrm{el}}$ are yours to
justify}, with a source; they are not Choupo data and this brief does not
supply them.

\subsection{The break-even, measured}

Running the case across the price grid gives a strictly linear response
(revenue is linear in the ammonia price, the raw-material bill linear in the
hydrogen price), so the break-even is exact and can be quoted:

{\small\begin{longtable}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Ammonia price} & \textbf{Break-even $p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$} &
  \textbf{Hydrogen bill at break-even} \\
\midrule
0.60\,EUR/kg & \measured{1.64\,EUR/kg} & \measured{297\,EUR/t\,NH$_3$} (49.5\,\% of revenue) \\

\section{Deliverables}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Reproduce the base case} and present the stream table, the
      per-unit and plant energy balances, and the mass closure. State the
      residuals; do not round them away.
\item \textbf{The design study.} Change the design decisions that are yours ---
      loop pressure, separator temperature, purge fraction, exchanger areas,
      the design pressures and residence times in \texttt{system/postDict} ---
      and report their effect on the fixed capital investment and on the
      specific power. At least one of your changes must make things
      \emph{worse}, and you must explain why.
\item \textbf{The hydrogen price study.} Report NPV against $p_{\mathrm{H_2}}$
      over the range 1.0 to 4.0\,EUR/kg, at both ammonia prices, and mark the
      break-even. Reproduce the two break-even values in this brief or explain
      your disagreement.
\item \textbf{Scenario I.} Choose $e$ and $c_{\mathrm{el}}$, cite your sources,
      and state the delivered electricity price at which this plant breaks
      even. Then say plainly whether that price is reachable at Sines and on
      what evidence.
\item \textbf{The honest limitations section.} Read the run's own caveat block
      --- Choupo prints every extrapolation, every unverified record and every
      assumed default at the end of the run --- and turn it into a section of
      your report. Two of them are named in \S6 below to start you off; the run
      lists \measured{32}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What this case does not model}

Stated here so you do not have to discover it in a viva.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{No credit is taken for anything the plant gives away.} The loop
      rejects \measured{18.3\,MW} at the water cooler and moves
      \measured{42.1\,MW} through the feed--effluent exchanger; a real plant
      exports part of that as steam. The oxygen by-product of electrolysis is
      outside the fence and is not sold. Both omissions push the appraisal
      \emph{down}.
\item \textbf{The utility bill is a declared lower bound.} The utility cost
      comes out at \measured{13.53 million EUR/yr}, and \measured{four}
      heat duties in this flowsheet reach no catalogue utility at all, so they
      are \emph{not} in it. Every figure derived from it --- COM$_d$, NPV,
      IRR --- inherits that bound, and the run says so by name.
\item \textbf{No regulatory or contractual framework} --- RFNBO, CBAM,
      contracts for difference, capital grants. None of it is thermodynamics
      and none of it belongs in \texttt{constant/economics}. If your appraisal
      depends on it, say so explicitly.
\item \textbf{The vessel sizer computes gas volumetric flow as $NRT/P$}, ideal
      gas, and states that in its own basis line. At 200\,bar in the synthesis
      loop that is the one place in the sizing chain where the stated basis and
      the case's thermodynamic package genuinely disagree. Quantifying the
      error is a legitimate --- and welcome --- finding for your report.
\item \textbf{SS316 is rated 100\,bar in the material record} and the Converter
      and Separator are designed at 240\,bar. Choupo warns per vessel and
      prices them anyway. Decide what to do about it and defend the decision.
\item \textbf{Two boundary streams carry a phase label the energy report's own
      equilibrium check disputes} --- \texttt{PURIFICATION.DryishGas}
      ($g(V{=}1) = -0.00138$, i.e.\ marginally below its dew point, which is
      what a flash's own vapour outlet is by construction) and
      \texttt{WashWater}. The report says so by name rather than absorbing it.
      Read the message; do not assume it names a defect in \emph{your} case.
\item \textbf{Construction is modelled as one year} even though
      \texttt{constructionPeriod 2} is declared; the whole investment is placed
      at $t=0$. Announced by the run, not silent.
\end{itemize}

\section{Running it}

\begin{verbatim}
source /path/to/Choupo/etc/bashrc
cd tutorials/plant/greenAmmoniaIndustrialN2
runCase -f .                 # solve, size, cost, appraise
choupoSolve --manifest .     # where each stream's state file belongs
\end{verbatim}

Outputs to read: \texttt{converged/} (the solved state, one file per stream),
\texttt{design/\allowbreak{}<SECTOR>/\allowbreak{}<unit>/\allowbreak{}<item>} (one specification sheet per physical
item), \texttt{reports/} (the balances, the utility allocation, the costing
tables). The sizing, costing and economics chain is driven by
\texttt{system/postDict} and priced by \texttt{constant/economics}; in both,
a unit or a stream must be named by its \textbf{engine} identity ---
\texttt{LOOP.Converter}, not \texttt{converter}; \texttt{ProductNH3}, not
\texttt{productNH3}.
